Inside Lagunas de Chacahua National Park

Located about 60 km west of Puerto Escondido, Oaxaca, Lagunas de Chacahua National Park has been a federally protected wetland since 1937. Five interconnected coastal lagoons framed by red and white mangrove forests create one of the Southern Mexican Pacific's richest wildlife sanctuaries.

Away from large resorts, Chacahua welcomes visitors into unhurried Afro-Oaxacan fishing communities, quiet wildlife corridors, and broad Pacific beaches where freshwater lagoons meet open surf.

What to Expect on the Mangrove Boat Cruise

After a comfortable 1-hour drive west to the lakeside village of Zapotal, you board a shaded motorboat for an unforgettable 2-hour journey across the national park:

  • Mangrove Labyrinths: Cruise smoothly beneath arching emerald mangrove canopies where roots shelter crabs, fish, and shorebirds.
  • Exotic & Migratory Birds: Spot roseate spoonbills, white egrets, brown pelicans, wood storks, and ospreys perched overhead.
  • Iguana Island: Visit wild riverbank trees dotted with large green iguanas basking in coastal sunlight, plus protected crocodile habitats nearby.

🌅 Chacahua Bay: Where the Lagoon Meets the Pacific

After crossing the wetlands, you step ashore in tranquil Chacahua village with generous free time to enjoy:

  • Swimming safely in the calm, warm water along the sheltered lagoon channel.
  • Watching the river mouth (bocabarra) where emerald lagoon currents swirl into turquoise Pacific surf.
  • Unwinding in beachfront hammocks and thatched palapas along miles of virgin sand.
  • Enjoying freshly caught grilled fish, shrimp tlayudas, and homemade salsas prepared by local village families (optional food purchase).

What Should You Bring to Chacahua?

  • Swimsuit worn under light breathable clothes, plus a beach towel.
  • Sandals or water shoes for walking easily along piers and sand.
  • Sunglasses, wide-brimmed sun hat, and biodegradable sunscreen.
  • Cash (Mexican Pesos): Very important — Chacahua has no ATMs, limited card terminals, and minimal cell signal, so bring cash for fresh food, coconuts, and cold drinks.

Frequently Asked Questions About Chacahua

How long does the Chacahua National Park tour last?

The complete experience lasts approximately 6 hours (plus scenic return transit), picking up around 12:30 PM and returning after sunset.

Can you swim safely at Chacahua?

Yes. Calm lagoon channels and shallow sandy coves near the bay provide safe, warm swimming conditions without heavy ocean waves.

What wild animals live inside Chacahua National Park?

Guests regularly see herons, pelicans, storks, roseate spoonbills, wild green iguanas in tree canopies, estuary crabs, and protected American crocodiles.

Is lunch or dinner included in the tour?

Meals are not pre-bundled so you can freely order fresh fish, seafood tlayudas, or vegetarian plates directly from local Chacahua family restaurants (bring cash).

Is the Chacahua tour comfortable for children and seniors?

Yes. Ground transport is air-conditioned, boats include shade canopies and mandatory life jackets, and protected lagoon channels have calm flat water.

When is the best season to visit Chacahua?

Chacahua shines year-round: November to May delivers sunny clear skies and calm emerald water, while June to October transforms the mangroves into lush vivid jungle.
