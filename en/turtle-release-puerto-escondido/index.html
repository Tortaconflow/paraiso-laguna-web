What Does the Sea Turtle Release Experience Involve?

The sea turtle release in Puerto Escondido is far more than a sightseeing tour: it is an opportunity to directly participate in the survival of endangered species. We visit community-managed conservation sanctuaries where Olive Ridley sea turtles nest along the pristine Oaxaca coast.

At the sanctuary, local marine biologists and conservationists explain the life cycle of sea turtles, the critical threats they face from poaching, climate change, and coastal pollution, and the success of community incubation corrals. Each guest receives a traditional biodegradable gourd shell containing an active newborn hatchling ready to imprint on the sand and head toward the waves.

La Escobilla Sanctuary & Community Conservation

The Oaxaca coast is internationally renowned as a premier sanctuary for sea turtle nesting. La Escobilla Sanctuary is celebrated worldwide as the epicenter of Olive Ridley turtles (Lepidochelys olivacea). Here, magnificent "arribadas" occur, where thousands of female turtles emerge simultaneously from the ocean to lay their eggs.

By joining our tour, your participation directly funds the maintenance of incubation corrals, 24/7 anti-poaching beach patrols, and fair local wages for native families protecting these fragile coastal habitats.

Ethical Guidelines for Sea Turtle Releases

To ensure our presence causes zero stress to the hatchlings and maximum ecological benefit, we uphold strict guidelines endorsed by certified marine biologists:

  • Natural Gourds Only: We never touch hatchlings with bare hands. Human body heat, sweat, and oils can stress the young turtles or transfer harmful bacteria.
  • Zero Chemical Lotions: Chemical sunscreens and bug sprays transfer to the sand and disrupt the delicate olfactory imprinting sensors turtles need to find their way back decades later.
  • Designated Pathways: Sanctuary biologists designate clear walking corridors to prevent stepping on natural underground nests.
  • No-Flash Photography: Camera flashes severely disorient newborn hatchlings, causing them to crawl away from the ocean waves toward artificial light.

🐢 Sea Turtle Species We Help Protect in Oaxaca

  • Olive Ridley Turtle (Golfina): The most abundant species on the Oaxaca coast, famous for synchronized mass nesting events.
  • Black Sea Turtle (Prieta): Regularly monitored along protected beaches of Oaxaca.
  • Leatherback Sea Turtle (Laúd): The largest sea turtle species in the world. Its nesting on local beaches is rare and strictly guarded.

Best Time of Year for the Sea Turtle Release Tour

While sanctuary monitoring continues year-round, peak hatchling releases align with the annual Pacific nesting cycle:

  • July to December: Peak season with hatchling releases practically every afternoon — the best window to witness mass emergences.
  • January to March: Late season with releases from remaining incubated nests on most evenings.
  • April to June: Low nesting activity. Message us on WhatsApp so we can confirm active hatchings before booking your date.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sea Turtle Release

When is sea turtle release season in Oaxaca?

The main nesting and release season runs from July through January, peaking between August and November when mass 'arribadas' bring hundreds of daily hatchlings.

Can children join the sea turtle release tour?

Yes, it is calm, gentle, and ideal for children of all ages and older travelers. The only physical activity is an easy walk on soft evening sand.

What happens after the baby turtles reach the ocean?

As hatchlings crawl across wet sand, they magnetically imprint on the beach's geographic coordinates. Roughly 15 to 20 years later, adult females navigate back to this exact coastline to nest.

How many baby turtles does each guest release?

Each guest receives one hatchling in an individual natural gourd bowl following strict no-touch handling rules led by local biologists.

Is this sea turtle release ethical and regulated?

Yes. We partner exclusively with federally authorized community sanctuaries supervised by trained conservation staff, ensuring every visit funds habitat protection.

What should I wear and avoid bringing?

Wear light, comfortable clothing and footwear easy to slip off on the sand. Please avoid chemical sunscreens or insect repellents before joining so residues never reach the hatchlings.
